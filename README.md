> A Multi-Paradigm Quantitative Analysis of Public Investments and Systemic Boundaries

## Executive Overview

This interactive notebook operationalizes and contrasts two fundamentally opposing economic evaluation paradigms:

### The Neoclassical MVPF Framework (Marginal Value of Public Funds): 

Measures the economic return of public spending based on subjective monetary willingness-to-pay () and long-run net tax recoupment under existing income distributions.

### The Balanced Framework (Steady-State Economics) (ESJ & Thermodynamics): 

Measures objective material throughput, thermodynamic work potential (EROI, Exergy), environmental limit compliance (Carbon budgets, Aquifer recharge, Biosphere integrity), and equitable elevation above the The Universal Baseline (Living Guarantee) social floor.
Core Philosophical & Institutional Conflict

### Money as a Stand-in for Human Needs: 

In neoclassical market economics, resource allocation is mediated through price signals and monetary willingness-to-pay (). Because willingness-to-pay is bounded by ability-to-pay, a wealthy individual's preference for luxury speed (e.g., private highway express toll lanes) is quantified as having vastly greater "social welfare" than an impoverished community's life-and-death requirement for clean water, transit, or housing.

### The Structural Distortion: 

When public policy, education, and courts use money as the yardstick for performance, human dignity is forced to compete against corporate capital. Fines punish poverty, resources flow to whoever holds the most liquidity, and policies encourage infinite material extraction on a finite biophysical planet.


### The Biophysical Alternative: 

Earth System Economics reverses this relationship. Essential social foundations (The Universal Baseline) are guaranteed as non-negotiable rights, upper-bound luxury footprints are constrained through fair-share caps (), and success is measured by the thermodynamic stability of the Coupled Human-Environment System (CHANS).
